# SpeakOut TV — Broadcast Brand System

## Master identity
**Name:** SpeakOut TV  
**Positioning:** The digital media network of SpeakOut Mental Health Outreach.  
**Primary line:** Real People. Real Conversations. Real Impact.  
**Brand idea:** More Than a Conversation.

## Broadcast architecture
1. **SpeakOut On The Move** — street and community conversations.
2. **SpeakOut Podcast** — studio and long-form interviews.
3. **How Are You, Really?** — concise wellbeing conversations.
4. **Youth Voices** — youth-led perspectives and discussions.
5. **Campus Connect** — schools, colleges and campus stories.
6. **Expert Corner** — informed educational conversations with qualified guests.
7. **SpeakOut Stories** — human and community stories.
8. **SpeakOut Special** — campaigns, events, live coverage and documentaries.

## Visual rules
- Master palette: deep navy, broadcast blue, SpeakOut orange, white.
- Orange is the active/live/attention color. Red is reserved for LIVE indicators.
- Thumbnails should use one dominant subject, a short 3–6 word headline, show badge and SpeakOut TV mark.
- Avoid dense text, generic stock-photo layouts and unverified health claims.
- Use 16:9 for episodes/YouTube, 9:16 for Shorts/Reels, and 1:1 only where a platform specifically benefits from it.

## Broadcast graphics package
- 3–5 second ident: dark screen → orange waveform → SPEAKOUT TV → tagline.
- Lower third: guest name on first line; verified role/organization on second line.
- LIVE bug: top-right red LIVE capsule + SpeakOut TV mark.
- End card: next episode / subscribe / visit SpeakOut TV.
- Transition: 0.5–1 second orange/blue waveform wipe.
- Watermark: small SpeakOut TV mark, top-right or bottom-right, low visual weight.

## Editorial safeguards
- Obtain appropriate consent before publishing interviews or identifiable participants.
- For children/minors, use the organization’s safeguarding and parent/school consent process.
- Do not present interviews as diagnosis or individual medical advice.
- Verify professional titles before displaying them on-screen.
- Avoid sensational mental-health framing and protect sensitive personal disclosures.
- Keep sponsor messages visually distinct from editorial content.

## Publishing workflow
Pitch → approve topic → consent/guest verification → record → edit → safeguarding/editorial review → thumbnail/title → upload video → publish in TV Admin → distribute clips → archive/report.

## Episode metadata
Every published episode should have: title, show, short description, YouTube URL, thumbnail, format, status, publish date, guest/presenter where relevant, and topic/category tags.

## Recommended launch sequence
Brand ident → channel trailer → host/presenter reveal → first On The Move → first studio podcast → short highlights → weekly programming rhythm.
