Learning Objectives

  • Identify features of a healthy relationship.
  • Practice stating a boundary.
  • Recognize warning signs of an unhealthy relationship.

Core Lesson

Healthy relationships make you feel respected, safe, valued, and able to be yourself. They do not require fear, control, humiliation, or constant pressure.

Boundaries are healthy limits. They help you protect your time, body, emotions, values, and safety.

Good communication includes listening, speaking honestly, using respectful language, and trying to understand before reacting.

Conflict is normal, but disrespect is not. Healthy conflict focuses on the issue, not insults, threats, or violence.

Reflection Questions

  1. What does respect look like in friendship?
  2. What boundary do you need to set?
  3. How can you handle conflict more calmly?

Practical Activity

Write down one boundary you would like to set in a relationship and one respectful way to say it.

Knowledge Check

  1. What distinguishes a healthy relationship from an unhealthy one?
  2. Is setting a boundary the same as rejecting someone?
  3. True or False: Refusing to accept a clearly stated boundary is a warning sign.

Key Takeaways

  • All relationships involve some conflict; what matters is how it is handled.
  • Boundaries are a normal, healthy part of any relationship.
  • Respectful communication includes listening and using "I feel" statements.
