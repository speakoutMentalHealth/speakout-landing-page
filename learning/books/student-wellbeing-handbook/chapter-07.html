Learning Objectives

  • Define resilience accurately.
  • Practice reframing a setback.
  • Identify one factor that strengthens resilience.

Core Lesson

Resilience is the ability to recover, adapt, and continue growing after challenges. It does not mean nothing hurts. It means you keep building strength.

Setbacks can teach us, but they should not define us. Failing an exam, losing a friendship, or making a mistake does not make your life a failure.

Resilient students use support, learn from experience, manage emotions, adjust plans, and keep hope alive.

Resilience grows through small habits: rest, reflection, problem-solving, gratitude, healthy relationships, and courage to try again.

Reflection Questions

  1. Describe a setback you survived.
  2. What helped you recover?
  3. What lesson did you learn from that experience?

Practical Activity

Think of a recent setback and write one thing it taught you, without minimizing how it felt.

Knowledge Check

  1. Does resilience mean never struggling?
  2. Name one factor that helps build resilience.
  3. True or False: Reframing a setback means pretending it did not hurt.

Key Takeaways

  • Resilience is about recovery and adaptation, not avoiding hardship.
  • Supportive relationships and self-compassion help build resilience.
  • Acknowledging a setback honestly is more sustainable than forcing positivity.
