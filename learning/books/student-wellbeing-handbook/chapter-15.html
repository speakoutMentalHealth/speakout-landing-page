Learning Objectives

  • Recognize signs that someone may need support.
  • Identify reasons students hesitate to ask for help.
  • Know who trusted people to reach out to are.

Core Lesson

Asking for help is not weakness. It is a wise step when a problem is too heavy, confusing, unsafe, or persistent.

You may need support if you feel hopeless, constantly anxious, unable to sleep, unable to function, unsafe, overwhelmed, or like hurting yourself or someone else.

Trusted support may include parents, guardians, teachers, counsellors, religious leaders, healthcare workers, or verified support services.

If there is immediate danger, contact emergency services or a responsible adult immediately. Do not wait alone in silence.

Reflection Questions

  1. What signs show someone may need help?
  2. Who are your trusted support people?
  3. What stops students from asking for help?

Practical Activity

Write down the names of two or three trusted people you could reach out to if you needed support.

Knowledge Check

  1. Is asking for help a sign of weakness or strength, according to this chapter?
  2. Name one sign that someone may need extra support.
  3. True or False: A problem must be "serious enough" before it is okay to ask for help.

Key Takeaways

  • Asking for help is a sign of self-awareness and strength.
  • Noticeable changes in mood, sleep or behaviour are worth paying attention to.
  • Support is available for a wide range of concerns, not only severe ones.
