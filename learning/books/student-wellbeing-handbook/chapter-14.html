Learning Objectives

  • Identify what genuine listening looks like.
  • Understand the limits of a friend's responsibility to "fix" a problem.
  • Know when a secret must be shared with an adult.

Core Lesson

Being a good friend does not mean solving everyone's problems. It means listening, caring, encouraging safe choices, and knowing when to involve help.

Active listening includes paying attention, not interrupting, asking gentle questions, and avoiding judgment.

Some issues are too serious to handle alone. If a friend talks about self-harm, abuse, violence, suicide, or serious danger, involve a trusted adult or professional immediately.

You can support others while still protecting your own wellbeing and boundaries.

Reflection Questions

  1. How can you listen better?
  2. What problem should never be kept secret?
  3. Who can help if a friend is unsafe?

Practical Activity

Think of a friend who might be going through something difficult, and consider one way you could listen better.

Knowledge Check

  1. What is the most valuable thing you can offer a struggling friend, according to this chapter?
  2. Should you ever keep a friend's disclosure of self-harm or suicidal thoughts secret?
  3. True or False: Supporting a friend means you should also look after your own wellbeing.

Key Takeaways

  • Genuine listening is often more valuable than having all the answers.
  • Some problems, like risk to safety, should never be kept secret.
  • Supporting a friend does not mean carrying their problem entirely alone.
