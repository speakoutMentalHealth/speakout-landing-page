Learning Objectives

  • Understand the main idea of this chapter.
  • Reflect on your own wellbeing experience.
  • Identify one practical action you can apply.

Core Lesson

Being a good friend does not mean solving everyone's problems. It means listening, caring, encouraging safe choices, and knowing when to involve help.

Active listening includes paying attention, not interrupting, asking gentle questions, and avoiding judgment.

Some issues are too serious to handle alone. If a friend talks about self-harm, abuse, violence, suicide, or serious danger, involve a trusted adult or professional immediately.

You can support others while still protecting your own wellbeing and boundaries.

Reflection Questions

  1. How can you listen better?
  2. What problem should never be kept secret?
  3. Who can help if a friend is unsafe?

Practical Activity

Complete a short reflection in your workbook. Write one action you will practice before the next lesson.

Knowledge Check

  1. What is one key idea from this chapter?
  2. Name one practical action you can take this week.
  3. True or False: Wellbeing grows through small consistent actions.

Key Takeaways

  • Wellbeing is practical and can be strengthened.
  • Small steps matter.
  • Support is part of growth.
