Learning Objectives

  • Recognize how social media affects mood.
  • Identify one digital habit to change.
  • Understand basic online safety practices.

Core Lesson

Technology can help you learn, connect, and create. It can also increase comparison, distraction, pressure, and exposure to harmful content.

Digital wellbeing means using technology in a way that supports your health, learning, relationships, and values.

Online pressure can make people compare their lives to edited versions of others. Remember that social media is often a highlight reel, not the full truth.

Healthy digital habits include screen breaks, privacy settings, reporting harmful content, avoiding late-night scrolling, and choosing content that helps you grow.

Reflection Questions

  1. How does social media affect your mood?
  2. What digital habit do you want to change?
  3. How can you protect yourself online?

Practical Activity

Notice how you feel during and after your next period of scrolling, and write down what you observe.

Knowledge Check

  1. What does social media typically show, according to this chapter?
  2. Name one reason screen use before bed can affect wellbeing.
  3. True or False: Cyberbullying should always be handled alone and never reported.

Key Takeaways

  • Social media often shows a curated highlight reel, not everyday reality.
  • Noticing how scrolling affects your mood is a useful digital wellbeing habit.
  • Protecting personal information and reporting cyberbullying both matter.
