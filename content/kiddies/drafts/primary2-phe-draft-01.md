## Facilitation, accessibility and safeguarding

Use fictional situations, drawings, paper cards and adult-led discussion. A learner may answer by speaking, pointing, writing, signing, dictation or an agreed communication aid; offer large print and plain-language restatements. Never require physical performance, touch, personal medical disclosure, recording, food disclosure, or public scoring. Adapt language and pace. A responsible adult checks local school procedures. These are educational discussions, not medical advice or first-aid certification. Stop any activity that feels unsafe and seek qualified help when needed.

## Physical & Health Education Lesson 1: Personal hygiene tools

**Aim:** Explain a safer or more inclusive choice in a fictional situation, giving one reason rather than memorising a button.

**Story:** A child is packing a school bag for the day.

**Model and discuss:** Personal hygiene tools such as toothbrushes should not be shared. A trusted adult can help arrange suitable supplies.

**Guided paper activity:** Sort fictional pictures into personal-use items and shared classroom items; explain one choice.

**Independent check:** Which item should not be shared between learners? Invite the learner to explain the reason through a comfortable response method. Then ask: Why ask an adult if a hygiene item is missing?

**Feedback and answer:** A personal toothbrush. They can help find a suitable safe option. Discuss why the alternatives could be unhelpful or unsafe. Accept a supported explanation; do not treat a multiple-choice score as mastery.

**Adaptation:** Read the story aloud, show a two-choice card, or offer additional thinking time. Record only anonymised instructional observations with school permission.

## Physical & Health Education Lesson 2: Water, movement and rest

**Aim:** Explain a safer or more inclusive choice in a fictional situation, giving one reason rather than memorising a button.

**Story:** Learners are playing a gentle game outdoors.

**Model and discuss:** During activity, people may need clean drinking water and rest. Needs differ, and adults should follow local safety arrangements.

**Guided paper activity:** Make a picture plan with a shaded pause place and a safe drinking-water point; do not prescribe an amount of water.

**Independent check:** What is a sensible choice when thirsty during play? Invite the learner to explain the reason through a comfortable response method. Then ask: Do all learners need identical breaks?

**Feedback and answer:** Ask for safe drinking water and a pause. No, people can need different amounts of rest. Discuss why the alternatives could be unhelpful or unsafe. Accept a supported explanation; do not treat a multiple-choice score as mastery.

**Adaptation:** Read the story aloud, show a two-choice card, or offer additional thinking time. Record only anonymised instructional observations with school permission.

## Physical & Health Education Lesson 3: Fair and accessible games

**Aim:** Explain a safer or more inclusive choice in a fictional situation, giving one reason rather than memorising a button.

**Story:** A class is choosing how to play a turn-taking game.

**Model and discuss:** Games can be adapted so classmates using different ways of moving or communicating can join. Winning is not the only goal.

**Guided paper activity:** Use paper tokens to demonstrate turns; propose a seated or pointing alternative without requiring physical performance.

**Independent check:** What helps a game include more learners? Invite the learner to explain the reason through a comfortable response method. Then ask: How should a class respond when a learner asks for a different way to join?

**Feedback and answer:** Offer accessible ways to take turns. Listen and agree on a safe adaptation. Discuss why the alternatives could be unhelpful or unsafe. Accept a supported explanation; do not treat a multiple-choice score as mastery.

**Adaptation:** Read the story aloud, show a two-choice card, or offer additional thinking time. Record only anonymised instructional observations with school permission.
