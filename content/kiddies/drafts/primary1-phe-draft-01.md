## Facilitation, accessibility and safeguarding

Use fictional situations, drawings, paper cards and adult-led discussion. A learner may answer by speaking, pointing, writing, signing, dictation or an agreed communication aid; offer large print and plain-language restatements. Never require physical performance, touch, personal medical disclosure, recording, food disclosure, or public scoring. Adapt language and pace. A responsible adult checks local school procedures. These are educational discussions, not medical advice or first-aid certification. Stop any activity that feels unsafe and seek qualified help when needed.

## Physical & Health Education Lesson 1: Listen to body signals

**Aim:** Explain a safer or more inclusive choice in a fictional situation, giving one reason rather than memorising a button.

**Story:** A learner feels tired during a classroom movement game.

**Model and discuss:** Feeling tired is a reason to pause and tell the supervising adult. Learners do not need to explain private health details.

**Guided paper activity:** Draw two picture cards: continue comfortably, or ask for a break. Discuss when a pause is helpful.

**Independent check:** What should a learner do when movement becomes uncomfortable? Invite the learner to explain the reason through a comfortable response method. Then ask: Why can pointing to a pause card be useful?

**Feedback and answer:** Ask the supervising adult for a break. It lets someone request a break without speaking. Discuss why the alternatives could be unhelpful or unsafe. Accept a supported explanation; do not treat a multiple-choice score as mastery.

**Adaptation:** Read the story aloud, show a two-choice card, or offer additional thinking time. Record only anonymised instructional observations with school permission.

## Physical & Health Education Lesson 2: Hands and everyday hygiene

**Aim:** Explain a safer or more inclusive choice in a fictional situation, giving one reason rather than memorising a button.

**Story:** Before eating, a child notices that their hands are dirty.

**Model and discuss:** Cleaning hands with soap and safe water, with suitable adult support, helps remove dirt and germs. A picture or explanation is enough for this lesson.

**Guided paper activity:** Sequence three drawn cards: wet hands, use soap and rub, rinse and dry appropriately. Ask an adult to check local water arrangements.

**Independent check:** When is handwashing especially useful? Invite the learner to explain the reason through a comfortable response method. Then ask: What is a safe classroom way to practise the steps?

**Feedback and answer:** Before eating and after using the toilet. Arrange drawn picture cards with an adult. Discuss why the alternatives could be unhelpful or unsafe. Accept a supported explanation; do not treat a multiple-choice score as mastery.

**Adaptation:** Read the story aloud, show a two-choice card, or offer additional thinking time. Record only anonymised instructional observations with school permission.

## Physical & Health Education Lesson 3: Choosing a safe play space

**Aim:** Explain a safer or more inclusive choice in a fictional situation, giving one reason rather than memorising a button.

**Story:** Two friends see loose objects across a walkway.

**Model and discuss:** Before play, look for obstacles and ask a responsible adult to make the space safe. Never ask a child to lift unknown or heavy objects.

**Guided paper activity:** Draw a safe route around a fictional obstacle and show where an adult can help.

**Independent check:** What should children do about an unknown object in a play area? Invite the learner to explain the reason through a comfortable response method. Then ask: Why is a clear route helpful?

**Feedback and answer:** Tell an adult and keep away. It helps different people move more safely. Discuss why the alternatives could be unhelpful or unsafe. Accept a supported explanation; do not treat a multiple-choice score as mastery.

**Adaptation:** Read the story aloud, show a two-choice card, or offer additional thinking time. Record only anonymised instructional observations with school permission.
