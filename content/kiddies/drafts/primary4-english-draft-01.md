# Primary 4 English Studies — Comparing Information and Revising Writing

> **Original editorial draft; not in My Learning.** Three sample lessons only; not a full term. Current NERDC edition, outcome codes, page references, class and term placement remain **unverified**. Independent qualified-teacher approval and supervised classroom pilot have not occurred.

## Accessible and safe delivery
Use large print, audio read-aloud, sign language, pointing, dictation or keyboard response. Offer a quiet alternative and time to reconsider. Learners may respond on paper without any device. Fictional examples avoid requests for children's personal information. Do not store names, photos, voice recordings, quiz results or public rankings. Feedback must explain reasoning rather than shame an incorrect answer.

## Lesson 1: Compare two accounts of one event
**Goal:** Identify a shared fact and a difference in perspective across two short original texts.

**Text A — class notice:** On Thursday, the class planted two young trees beside the playground. The school caretaker watered them.

**Text B — fictional pupil journal:** Thursday's tree planting was exciting. I liked carrying the small watering can. I hope the trees grow tall enough to give shade.

**Facilitator model:** Both texts refer to planting trees on Thursday. The notice reports observable events; the journal shares a feeling and a hope. Do not treat a person's hope as a proven fact.

**Interactive practice (digital choice or paper cards):**
1. What do both texts mention? A. Tree planting on Thursday. B. A football match. C. A rainy holiday.
2. Which statement is a personal feeling? A. The caretaker watered the trees. B. The planting was exciting. C. Two trees were planted.

**Feedback and worked answers:** 1=A because both texts describe the same event. 2=B because “exciting” expresses the writer's feeling. Invite the learner to point to the supporting words.

**Printable worksheet:** Make a two-column table headed “Both texts” and “Only the journal.” Add one example to each. Write one respectful question you would ask the journal writer.

## Lesson 2: Separate fact, opinion and prediction
**Goal:** Classify simple statements and justify each classification.

**Original mini-report:** The school library has four reading tables. Some pupils think the blue chairs are the most comfortable. The librarian plans to add a new shelf next term.

**Facilitator model:** A fact can be checked, an opinion expresses a judgement, and a prediction says what may happen. A plan is not proof that an event has happened.

**Interactive practice (digital choice or paper cards):**
1. “The library has four reading tables” is: A. Fact. B. Opinion. C. Prediction.
2. “Blue chairs are the most comfortable” is: A. Fact. B. Opinion. C. Prediction.

**Feedback and worked answers:** 1=A: tables can be counted. 2=B: comfort varies between people. Discuss that the proposed shelf is a plan about the future, not an already verified addition.

**Printable worksheet:** Label each as fact, opinion or future plan: (i) Four reading tables are in the library. (ii) The chairs look beautiful. (iii) The librarian plans a shelf. Write one checkable fact about the fictional report.

## Lesson 3: Revise a paragraph for clarity
**Goal:** Improve a short paragraph using sentence boundaries, precise vocabulary and a logical order.

**Original draft paragraph:** our group made a garden sign it was nice we wrote keep the garden clean then we put it by the gate

**Facilitator model:** Separate ideas into sentences, capitalise the first word and add punctuation. Replace vague “nice” with a more informative detail only if the text provides evidence; do not invent a colour or size.

**Interactive practice (digital choice or paper cards):**
1. Which revision is clearer? A. Our group made a garden sign. We wrote “Keep the garden clean.” Then we placed it by the gate. B. our group garden gate clean. C. Our group made a garden sign it was nice we wrote.
2. Which word best indicates the last action? A. Before. B. Then. C. Yesterday.

**Feedback and worked answers:** 1=A: it uses complete sentences and keeps the events in order. 2=B: “Then” links the final action to the previous step. Encourage learners to explain why an edit improves meaning.

**Printable worksheet:** Rewrite the draft as three complete sentences. Underline the instruction written on the sign. Explain one change you made and why.

## Printable assessment sheet (offline alternative)
Optional anonymous learner code: ______

1. Identify one detail common to both tree-planting texts. ______
2. Classify “The blue chairs are the best” and explain your choice. ______
3. Rewrite: our class cleaned the reading corner then we arranged the books ______
4. Choose a response mode: oral / signed / pointed / dictated / written.

## Facilitator answer key and checks
1. Both texts mention tree planting on Thursday.
2. Opinion; “best” is a personal judgement without an agreed measure.
3. “Our class cleaned the reading corner. Then we arranged the books.” Accept equally clear punctuation.
4. Response modes have equal standing; assess understanding, not the mode.
