# Primary 3 English Studies — Term 1 extended original draft

**Editorial status:** Development draft only. Six original sample lessons; NOT a complete term or verified NERDC outcome map. Not approved by a qualified teacher or tested with learners.

**Access and safeguarding:** No child names, scores, audio recordings or identifiers are collected. All activities work on paper or orally. The named characters are fictional.

## Lesson 1 — Find a main idea

**Intended skill:** Find a main idea. Read, listen to or examine the original passage and explain an answer using its wording.

**Original reading / listening text:** Our class started a reading corner. Each learner brought a story or made a picture book. We arranged the books on a low shelf. Now we can read during quiet time.

**Facilitator sequence:** Ask for one supporting sentence from the text. Invite individual thinking, paired discussion and non-competitive feedback. Model one response before independent practice.

**Interactive practice prompt:** What is the paragraph mainly about?

**Answer / evidence:** The class making a reading corner.

**Printable worksheet:** (1) What is the paragraph mainly about? (2) Give the paragraph a title; copy one detail that supports your title. (3) Highlight or dictate a word or sentence from the passage that supports your response.

**Marking guidance:** Credit the stated answer and other evidence-based equivalents; give feedback on reasoning, not reading speed. For open-ended writing, check clarity, relevant details and appropriate punctuation rather than requiring identical wording.

**Accessibility and extension:** Offer large print, spoken response, reading partner, pointing, typing or handwriting. Learners may use a picture or sentence frame; confident learners explain a second interpretation supported by the text.

## Lesson 6 — Write an observation

**Intended skill:** Write an observation. Read, listen to or examine the original passage and explain an answer using its wording.

**Original reading / listening text:** At the school garden, we saw two butterflies near a flowering shrub. The soil was damp after rain.

**Facilitator sequence:** Contrast 'I saw' with 'I think'. Invite individual thinking, paired discussion and non-competitive feedback. Model one response before independent practice.

**Interactive practice prompt:** Give one observation, not a guess.

**Answer / evidence:** The soil was damp / two butterflies were seen.

**Printable worksheet:** (1) Give one observation, not a guess. (2) Write three observed facts about a safe object or picture; avoid guesses. (3) Highlight or dictate a word or sentence from the passage that supports your response.

**Marking guidance:** Credit the stated answer and other evidence-based equivalents; give feedback on reasoning, not reading speed. For open-ended writing, check clarity, relevant details and appropriate punctuation rather than requiring identical wording.

**Accessibility and extension:** Offer large print, spoken response, reading partner, pointing, typing or handwriting. Learners may use a picture or sentence frame; confident learners explain a second interpretation supported by the text.
