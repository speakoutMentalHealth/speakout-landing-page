# Primary 2 English Studies — Term 1 extended original draft

**Editorial status:** Development draft only. Six original sample lessons; NOT a complete term or verified NERDC outcome map. Not approved by a qualified teacher or tested with learners.

**Access and safeguarding:** No child names, scores, audio recordings or identifiers are collected. All activities work on paper or orally. The named characters are fictional.

## Lesson 1 — Read a short account

**Intended skill:** Read a short account. Read, listen to or examine the original passage and explain an answer using its wording.

**Original reading / listening text:** At sunrise, Zainab carried water to the garden. She watered three young plants. By afternoon, their leaves stood upright.

**Facilitator sequence:** Read the account twice; invite a learner to point to evidence. Invite individual thinking, paired discussion and non-competitive feedback. Model one response before independent practice.

**Interactive practice prompt:** When did Zainab water the plants?

**Answer / evidence:** At sunrise.

**Printable worksheet:** (1) When did Zainab water the plants? (2) Write the events in order: leaves upright / water plants / carry water. (3) Highlight or dictate a word or sentence from the passage that supports your response.

**Marking guidance:** Credit the stated answer and other evidence-based equivalents; give feedback on reasoning, not reading speed. For open-ended writing, check clarity, relevant details and appropriate punctuation rather than requiring identical wording.

**Accessibility and extension:** Offer large print, spoken response, reading partner, pointing, typing or handwriting. Learners may use a picture or sentence frame; confident learners explain a second interpretation supported by the text.

## Lesson 6 — Friendly note

**Intended skill:** Friendly note. Read, listen to or examine the original passage and explain an answer using its wording.

**Original reading / listening text:** Dear Musa, Thank you for lending me your pencil. I returned it to your desk. From, Sade.

**Facilitator sequence:** Identify greeting, message and closing without asking for real names. Invite individual thinking, paired discussion and non-competitive feedback. Model one response before independent practice.

**Interactive practice prompt:** Why did Sade write to Musa?

**Answer / evidence:** To thank him for lending a pencil.

**Printable worksheet:** (1) Why did Sade write to Musa? (2) Write a short thank-you note using invented names and a polite closing. (3) Highlight or dictate a word or sentence from the passage that supports your response.

**Marking guidance:** Credit the stated answer and other evidence-based equivalents; give feedback on reasoning, not reading speed. For open-ended writing, check clarity, relevant details and appropriate punctuation rather than requiring identical wording.

**Accessibility and extension:** Offer large print, spoken response, reading partner, pointing, typing or handwriting. Learners may use a picture or sentence frame; confident learners explain a second interpretation supported by the text.
