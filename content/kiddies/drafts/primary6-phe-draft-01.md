# Primary 6 Physical & Health Education — Responsible Choices

> **Editorial draft — not published to learners.** Original SpeakOut content awaiting independent teacher review. Detailed NERDC edition, term, page and outcome alignment: **unverified**. No learner pilot or school sign-off is claimed.

## Facilitator safeguards and access

All situations are fictional. Learners may speak, sign, point, type, write or dictate, and may opt out of personal discussion. Offer large print, plain-language summaries, extra time and offline paper equivalents. Never require touching, strenuous activity, medical disclosure, recording, or public ranking. Follow school safeguarding policies. Seek qualified medical assistance for concerning symptoms; this lesson is not diagnosis or treatment advice.

## Lesson 1: Evaluating health information

**Goal:** Evaluate a fictional choice, give a reason and identify a safe alternative.

**Scenario:** A group chat circulates an unsupported claim that one drink prevents all illness.

**Facilitator explanation:** Health claims need trustworthy evidence; ask a qualified adult or healthcare professional rather than forwarding rumours.

**Offline guided activity:** Compare fictional statements: evidence-based advice versus an unsupported miracle claim.

**Interactive practice (oral, paper or digital prompt):** What should you do before sharing a surprising health claim? Allow response selection and explanation; offer neutral retry feedback without collecting identifying information.

**Answer and reasoning:** Check a reliable source and ask a qualified adult; avoid spreading unsupported claims. Discuss alternatives respectfully.

**Adaptation:** Offer picture cards, text-to-speech or a dictated answer; no physical demonstration is needed.

## Lesson 2: Responding to peer pressure

**Goal:** Evaluate a fictional choice, give a reason and identify a safe alternative.

**Scenario:** Friends encourage a learner to try an unsafe substance.

**Facilitator explanation:** A learner can refuse, leave and tell a trusted adult; avoid shaming anyone who needs help.

**Offline guided activity:** Write a short fictional refusal and identify a safe support person.

**Interactive practice (oral, paper or digital prompt):** What are two safe responses to pressure to use an unknown substance? Allow response selection and explanation; offer neutral retry feedback without collecting identifying information.

**Answer and reasoning:** Refuse and seek support from a trusted adult. Discuss alternatives respectfully.

**Adaptation:** Offer picture cards, text-to-speech or a dictated answer; no physical demonstration is needed.

## Lesson 3: Designing an inclusive recreation plan

**Goal:** Evaluate a fictional choice, give a reason and identify a safe alternative.

**Scenario:** A class must plan a game for learners with varied mobility and communication needs.

**Facilitator explanation:** Invite preferences, provide accessible roles and allow rest without requiring anyone to disclose a diagnosis.

**Offline guided activity:** Create a paper recreation plan with choice of roles, rest options and adult supervision.

**Interactive practice (oral, paper or digital prompt):** What makes a recreation plan fair and accessible? Allow response selection and explanation; offer neutral retry feedback without collecting identifying information.

**Answer and reasoning:** Different ways to participate, learner choice, rest and supervision. Discuss alternatives respectfully.

**Adaptation:** Offer picture cards, text-to-speech or a dictated answer; no physical demonstration is needed.
