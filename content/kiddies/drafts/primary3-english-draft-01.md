# Primary 3 English Studies — Reading for Meaning and Clear Writing

> **Original editorial draft; not in My Learning.** These are three sample lessons, not a complete term. The applicable NERDC edition, class-specific outcomes, identifiers, pages and term sequence are **unverified**. No independent teacher approval or supervised learner pilot has occurred.

## Inclusive facilitation and safeguarding
Allow a learner to listen, read large print, sign, point, dictate or type. Give extra time and repeat instructions without penalty. Questions can be played orally or with paper cards; no account, camera, child name, personal story, score upload or public ranking is required. A facilitator should check comprehension and adapt the text, not infer mastery from one quiz. All people and events below are fictional.

## Lesson 1: Find a clue and make an inference
**Goal:** Distinguish what a short passage states from a reasonable inference supported by a clue.

**Original passage:** On Monday, Kemi carried a folded umbrella to school. The sky was grey. When the first drops fell, she opened it and walked to the classroom without getting wet.

**Facilitator model:** The text says the sky was grey. I infer that Kemi expected rain because she brought an umbrella. An inference uses a clue; it is not a wild guess.

**Interactive practice (digital choice or paper cards):**
1. Why might Kemi have carried the umbrella? A. She expected rain. B. She wanted to sweep the floor. C. She had lost her bag.
2. Which clue best supports the answer? A. The sky was grey. B. It was Monday. C. She walked to class.

**Feedback and worked answers:** 1=A: the umbrella was useful when rain began. 2=A: grey clouds suggest possible rain; the day of the week does not. If a learner chooses another option, reread the clue and try again.

**Printable worksheet:** Underline the clue that suggests rain. Complete: “I think Kemi expected ___ because ___.” Draw one other safe item to carry on a rainy day and explain why.

## Lesson 2: Put a paragraph in logical order
**Goal:** Sequence four related actions and write a connected paragraph with time words.

**Original sentence cards:** (A) Finally, the class displayed the finished poster. (B) First, Bayo drew a large tree. (C) Next, Lara coloured the leaves. (D) Then, they wrote a title above the tree.

**Facilitator model:** A clear paragraph begins with the first action, follows the steps, and finishes with the result. Time words help the reader.

**Interactive practice (digital choice or paper cards):**
1. Which order makes sense? A. B-C-D-A. B. A-B-C-D. C. D-A-C-B.
2. Which word shows the last step? A. Next. B. Finally. C. First.

**Feedback and worked answers:** 1=A: drawing comes before colouring, labelling and displaying. 2=B: “Finally” signals the end. Accept equivalent chronological explanations.

**Printable worksheet:** Number the four cards 1–4. Rewrite them as a four-sentence paragraph. Circle the time words and add a suitable title.

## Lesson 3: Use speech marks and punctuation
**Goal:** Recognise and write a short piece of dialogue using a capital letter, speech marks and appropriate ending punctuation.

**Original dialogue:** “May I borrow your ruler?” asked Sade. “Yes, please return it after the lesson,” replied Nura.

**Facilitator model:** Speech marks show the exact words spoken. A question ends with a question mark inside the closing speech mark. Names begin with capital letters.

**Interactive practice (digital choice or paper cards):**
1. Which is correctly punctuated? A. “Where is my book?” asked Sade. B. “where is my book” asked Sade. C. Where is my book asked Sade.
2. Which mark shows that someone is asking? A. Full stop. B. Question mark. C. Comma.

**Feedback and worked answers:** 1=A: it has an opening capital, speech marks and a question mark. 2=B. Read the sentence aloud with question intonation and allow a retry.

**Printable worksheet:** Correct: where is the library asked bayo. Write a polite two-line conversation between two fictional classmates about borrowing a pencil.

## Printable assessment sheet (offline alternative)
Learner code (optional, not identifying): ______   Date (optional): ______

1. In the umbrella story, name one clue and one inference. ______
2. Order these steps: display poster; draw tree; write title; colour leaves. ______
3. Add punctuation: may i join the game asked ada ______
4. Choose how to respond: speaking / signing / pointing / dictating / writing.

## Facilitator answer key and checks
1. Clue: grey sky or falling drops; inference: Kemi expected or prepared for rain. Distinguish evidence from inference.
2. Draw tree → colour leaves → write title → display poster.
3. “May I join the game?” asked Ada. Accept equivalent punctuation appropriate to the local style guide.
4. Every response mode is valid; do not grade the mode itself.
