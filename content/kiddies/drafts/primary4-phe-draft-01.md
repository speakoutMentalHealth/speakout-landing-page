## Facilitation, safeguarding and accessibility

Use fictional scenarios only. Learners may speak, point, sign, write or dictate; provide plain language, large print and rest breaks. No compulsory physical demonstration, touch, medical disclosure, photographs or identifiable learner records. Follow school safeguarding rules; refer urgent concerns to qualified adults. This material is not clinical advice.

## Lesson 1: Planning safe activity

**Learning goal:** Explain a safe, respectful or inclusive decision and give a reason.

**Scenario:** A class plans a short recreation session on a warm day.

**Teacher explanation:** A responsible adult should check space, weather, water and accessible rest choices before activity.

**Guided offline activity:** Create a four-part paper checklist: space, adult, water, rest.

**Interactive practice prompt (paper or accessible digital choice):** Who should check whether the play space is safe? Invite the learner to choose a response and explain why; give supportive feedback without ranking children.

**Facilitator feedback:** A responsible supervising adult. Discuss other responses sensitively and allow retries.

**Accessibility:** Offer a spoken, written, signed, pictorial or dictated response with extra time.

## Lesson 2: Recognising unsafe pressure

**Learning goal:** Explain a safe, respectful or inclusive decision and give a reason.

**Scenario:** Friends dare someone to try an unfamiliar risky stunt.

**Teacher explanation:** Declining a risky dare and seeking adult support is a sensible choice; nobody owes a physical demonstration.

**Guided offline activity:** Write a fictional respectful refusal and a safer group alternative.

**Interactive practice prompt (paper or accessible digital choice):** What can a learner say to an unsafe dare? Invite the learner to choose a response and explain why; give supportive feedback without ranking children.

**Facilitator feedback:** No, and suggest a safer option or ask an adult. Discuss other responses sensitively and allow retries.

**Accessibility:** Offer a spoken, written, signed, pictorial or dictated response with extra time.

## Lesson 3: Fair rules for inclusive games

**Learning goal:** Explain a safe, respectful or inclusive decision and give a reason.

**Scenario:** A classroom game excludes a learner who communicates by pointing.

**Teacher explanation:** Agree clear rules and multiple ways to respond so everyone can participate.

**Guided offline activity:** Rewrite two fictional game rules to allow pointing or speaking.

**Interactive practice prompt (paper or accessible digital choice):** What makes a game rule more inclusive? Invite the learner to choose a response and explain why; give supportive feedback without ranking children.

**Facilitator feedback:** Allow different safe ways to take part. Discuss other responses sensitively and allow retries.

**Accessibility:** Offer a spoken, written, signed, pictorial or dictated response with extra time.
