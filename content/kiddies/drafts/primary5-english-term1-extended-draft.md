# Primary 5 English Studies — Term 1 extended original draft

**Editorial status:** Development draft only. Six original sample lessons; NOT a complete term or verified NERDC outcome map. Not approved by a qualified teacher or tested with learners.

**Access and safeguarding:** No child names, scores, audio recordings or identifiers are collected. All activities work on paper or orally. The named characters are fictional.

## Lesson 1 — Summarise a report

**Intended skill:** Summarise a report. Read, listen to or examine the original passage and explain an answer using its wording.

**Original reading / listening text:** A school club placed labelled bins near the classrooms. At first, learners mixed paper with food scraps. Club members explained the labels and demonstrated sorting. After two weeks, more paper reached the recycling bin.

**Facilitator sequence:** Ask learners to distinguish problem, action and result. Invite individual thinking, paired discussion and non-competitive feedback. Model one response before independent practice.

**Interactive practice prompt:** What changed after the club's explanation?

**Answer / evidence:** More paper was sorted into the recycling bin.

**Printable worksheet:** (1) What changed after the club's explanation? (2) Summarise the report in two sentences without copying the whole passage. (3) Highlight or dictate a word or sentence from the passage that supports your response.

**Marking guidance:** Credit the stated answer and other evidence-based equivalents; give feedback on reasoning, not reading speed. For open-ended writing, check clarity, relevant details and appropriate punctuation rather than requiring identical wording.

**Accessibility and extension:** Offer large print, spoken response, reading partner, pointing, typing or handwriting. Learners may use a picture or sentence frame; confident learners explain a second interpretation supported by the text.

## Lesson 6 — Persuasive paragraph

**Intended skill:** Persuasive paragraph. Read, listen to or examine the original passage and explain an answer using its wording.

**Original reading / listening text:** Our school should keep a reading corner. Books allow pupils to explore new ideas. A quiet place also helps readers concentrate. We can care for the books by returning them after use.

**Facilitator sequence:** Separate claim, evidence and a suggested action. Invite individual thinking, paired discussion and non-competitive feedback. Model one response before independent practice.

**Interactive practice prompt:** State the claim and one reason.

**Answer / evidence:** Claim: keep a reading corner; reason: books explore ideas / quiet helps concentration.

**Printable worksheet:** (1) State the claim and one reason. (2) Write a four-sentence argument for a safe school improvement, including a counterpoint. (3) Highlight or dictate a word or sentence from the passage that supports your response.

**Marking guidance:** Credit the stated answer and other evidence-based equivalents; give feedback on reasoning, not reading speed. For open-ended writing, check clarity, relevant details and appropriate punctuation rather than requiring identical wording.
