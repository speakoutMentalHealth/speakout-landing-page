## Term 1 · Lesson 1: Plant parts

**Learning aim:** Recognise roots, stems and leaves using a picture.

**Preparation (5 minutes):** The facilitator reads the original scenario aloud, offers a large-print copy or illustration, and invites learners to describe what they notice. Avoid assessing speed, speech or handwriting.

**Model and discussion (10 minutes):** A drawing shows roots under soil, a stem above soil and leaves attached to the stem. Invite a learner to restate the observation, and model a respectful response to a different explanation. Ask which evidence supports the answer.

**Interactive practice (5 minutes):** Which part commonly takes up water from soil?

**Answer and feedback:** Roots. Invite the learner to explain the answer in their own words, point to a drawing or respond through an assistive communication method. If unsure, revisit the model rather than marking a permanent failure.

**Printable worksheet (10 minutes):** Label roots, stem and leaves on a plant drawing. Include a labelled picture or two sentences explaining the choice; allow dictation or oral response.

**Facilitator check:** Can the learner identify the key feature and explain a reason? Record only an anonymous classroom-level observation if permitted; no learner names or accounts required.

**Safety and accessibility:** Use illustrations rather than uprooting plants. Provide high-contrast text, spoken instructions, rest breaks and a paper alternative. All participation is optional and supervised.

**Extension:** Ask the learner to invent one additional safe example and explain how it connects to the lesson aim.

## Term 3 · Lesson 1: Water in daily life

**Learning aim:** Identify safe everyday uses of water and conservation.

**Preparation (5 minutes):** The facilitator reads the original scenario aloud, offers a large-print copy or illustration, and invites learners to describe what they notice. Avoid assessing speed, speech or handwriting.

**Model and discussion (10 minutes):** A tap drips after washing hands. A pupil asks an adult to close it. Invite a learner to restate the observation, and model a respectful response to a different explanation. Ask which evidence supports the answer.

**Interactive practice (5 minutes):** How can we avoid wasting clean water?

**Answer and feedback:** Close taps properly and report leaks to an adult. Invite the learner to explain the answer in their own words, point to a drawing or respond through an assistive communication method. If unsure, revisit the model rather than marking a permanent failure.

**Printable worksheet (10 minutes):** Draw two ways to save water at school. Include a labelled picture or two sentences explaining the choice; allow dictation or oral response.

**Facilitator check:** Can the learner identify the key feature and explain a reason? Record only an anonymous classroom-level observation if permitted; no learner names or accounts required.

**Safety and accessibility:** Do not drink water from unknown sources. Provide high-contrast text, spoken instructions, rest breaks and a paper alternative. All participation is optional and supervised.

**Extension:** Ask the learner to invent one additional safe example and explain how it connects to the lesson aim.
