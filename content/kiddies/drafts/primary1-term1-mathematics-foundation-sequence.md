# Primary 1 — Term 1 mathematics foundation sequence (draft)

**Release status:** Original provisional teaching material. Four sequenced units, not a complete term or an approved NERDC-mapped scheme. School scheme reconciliation, teacher review and learner pilot are pending.

**Suggested pacing:** One unit per teaching week, adaptable to learner needs; pacing is not an official school timetable. Use local examples without requiring personal data.

## Unit 1: Counting 0–20

**Observable objective:** By the end, the learner can answer the focus question and demonstrate the idea with objects, drawing, speech or writing.

**Starter (5 minutes):** Ask what learners already know about counting 0–20; show a familiar safe object, picture or short written example.

**Teacher modelling (10 minutes):** Use a ten-frame and touch each object while saying its number. Think aloud through one example before asking pupils to practise independently.

**Guided practice (10 minutes):** Count twelve bottle tops or drawn circles. Point to each exactly once. Invite different strategies, including speaking, pointing and writing.

**Independent worksheet (10 minutes):** Draw 12 dots and circle groups of 5; write the numeral. Include space for a learner drawing or spoken response recorded by the facilitator.

**Answer / success criterion:** 12. Accept equivalent accurate explanations; check both the answer and reasoning.

**Misconception to check:** Counting objects twice or skipping an object.

**Exit question:** Count twelve bottle tops or drawn circles. Point to each exactly once. A learner should explain or demonstrate the answer without public ranking.

**Home/offline alternative:** Use paper drawings or safe household objects; participation must not depend on internet access or paid materials.

**Accessibility and safeguarding:** Large-print copies, oral or non-writing responses, clear instructions, no timed scoring, no identifying learner data. Supervise all physical activities and respect individual ability.

## Unit 2: Comparing quantities

**Observable objective:** By the end, the learner can answer the focus question and demonstrate the idea with objects, drawing, speech or writing.

**Starter (5 minutes):** Ask what learners already know about comparing quantities; show a familiar safe object, picture or short written example.

**Teacher modelling (10 minutes):** Arrange objects in matched pairs before counting extras. Think aloud through one example before asking pupils to practise independently.

**Guided practice (10 minutes):** A basket has 8 oranges and another has 5. Which has more and by how many? Invite different strategies, including speaking, pointing and writing.

**Independent worksheet (10 minutes):** Draw both groups; cross out matching pairs; write 8 − 5 = __. Include space for a learner drawing or spoken response recorded by the facilitator.

**Answer / success criterion:** 8 has 3 more. Accept equivalent accurate explanations; check both the answer and reasoning.

**Misconception to check:** Guessing from the arrangement rather than counting.

**Exit question:** A basket has 8 oranges and another has 5. Which has more and by how many? A learner should explain or demonstrate the answer without public ranking.

## Unit 4: Subtraction stories

**Observable objective:** By the end, the learner can answer the focus question and demonstrate the idea with objects, drawing, speech or writing.

**Starter (5 minutes):** Ask what learners already know about subtraction stories; show a familiar safe object, picture or short written example.

**Teacher modelling (10 minutes):** Act out removing four counters from ten. Think aloud through one example before asking pupils to practise independently.

**Guided practice (10 minutes):** There are 10 birds. Four fly away. How many remain? Invite different strategies, including speaking, pointing and writing.

**Independent worksheet (10 minutes):** Draw 10 birds, cross out 4, write 10 − 4 = __. Include space for a learner drawing or spoken response recorded by the facilitator.

**Answer / success criterion:** 6 birds. Accept equivalent accurate explanations; check both the answer and reasoning.

**Misconception to check:** Adding instead of removing.

**Exit question:** There are 10 birds. Four fly away. How many remain? A learner should explain or demonstrate the answer without public ranking.

**Home/offline alternative:** Use paper drawings or safe household objects; participation must not depend on internet access or paid materials.
