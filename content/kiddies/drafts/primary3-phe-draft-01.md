## Facilitation, safeguarding and accessibility

Use fictional scenarios only. Learners may speak, point, sign, write or dictate; provide plain language, large print and rest breaks. No compulsory physical demonstration, touch, medical disclosure, photographs or identifiable learner records. Follow school safeguarding rules; refer urgent concerns to qualified adults. This material is not clinical advice.

## Lesson 1: Respecting personal boundaries

**Learning goal:** Explain a safe, respectful or inclusive decision and give a reason.

**Scenario:** During a team activity, one child does not want to be touched.

**Teacher explanation:** Ask permission before contact; respect a refusal and offer a no-contact alternative.

**Guided offline activity:** Design a paper game where players pass written messages rather than touching.

**Interactive practice prompt (paper or accessible digital choice):** What should a teammate do after hearing 'no' to touch? Invite the learner to choose a response and explain why; give supportive feedback without ranking children.

**Facilitator feedback:** Respect the choice and use a no-contact option. Discuss other responses sensitively and allow retries.

**Accessibility:** Offer a spoken, written, signed, pictorial or dictated response with extra time.

## Lesson 2: Clean shared spaces

**Learning goal:** Explain a safe, respectful or inclusive decision and give a reason.

**Scenario:** After art class, paper scraps cover the floor.

**Teacher explanation:** Keep shared spaces tidy with safe adult guidance; never pick up sharp or unknown waste.

**Guided offline activity:** Draw a before-and-after classroom plan and mark items for adult handling.

**Interactive practice prompt (paper or accessible digital choice):** What should learners do about an unknown sharp object? Invite the learner to choose a response and explain why; give supportive feedback without ranking children.

**Facilitator feedback:** Keep away and tell a responsible adult. Discuss other responses sensitively and allow retries.

**Accessibility:** Offer a spoken, written, signed, pictorial or dictated response with extra time.

## Lesson 3: Movement at a comfortable pace

**Learning goal:** Explain a safe, respectful or inclusive decision and give a reason.

**Scenario:** A fictional pupil needs more time in a movement game.

**Teacher explanation:** Different people move differently; fair games can offer seated, slower or pointing options.

**Guided offline activity:** Invent two versions of a paper-token movement game.

**Interactive practice prompt (paper or accessible digital choice):** How can a group include a pupil who needs more time? Invite the learner to choose a response and explain why; give supportive feedback without ranking children.

**Facilitator feedback:** Offer an accessible pace or alternative without pressure. Discuss other responses sensitively and allow retries.

**Accessibility:** Offer a spoken, written, signed, pictorial or dictated response with extra time.
