## Term 1 · Lesson 1: Living and non-living things

**Learning aim:** Identify visible differences between living and non-living things.

**Preparation (5 minutes):** The facilitator reads the original scenario aloud, offers a large-print copy or illustration, and invites learners to describe what they notice. Avoid assessing speed, speech or handwriting.

**Model and discussion (10 minutes):** A tree grows and needs water; a stone does not grow or need water. Invite a learner to restate the observation, and model a respectful response to a different explanation. Ask which evidence supports the answer.

**Interactive practice (5 minutes):** Is a tree living? Explain using one observable characteristic.

**Answer and feedback:** Yes. A tree grows and needs water. Invite the learner to explain the answer in their own words, point to a drawing or respond through an assistive communication method. If unsure, revisit the model rather than marking a permanent failure.

**Printable worksheet (10 minutes):** Draw one living and one non-living thing and label one difference. Include a labelled picture or two sentences explaining the choice; allow dictation or oral response.

**Facilitator check:** Can the learner identify the key feature and explain a reason? Record only an anonymous classroom-level observation if permitted; no learner names or accounts required.

**Safety and accessibility:** Do not pick plants or disturb animals. Provide high-contrast text, spoken instructions, rest breaks and a paper alternative. All participation is optional and supervised.

**Extension:** Ask the learner to invent one additional safe example and explain how it connects to the lesson aim.

## Term 2 · Lesson 1: Our senses and safe observation

**Learning aim:** Use sight, hearing and touch to describe familiar objects safely.

**Preparation (5 minutes):** The facilitator reads the original scenario aloud, offers a large-print copy or illustration, and invites learners to describe what they notice. Avoid assessing speed, speech or handwriting.

**Model and discussion (10 minutes):** An adult places a clean, smooth book and a soft cloth on a table. Invite a learner to restate the observation, and model a respectful response to a different explanation. Ask which evidence supports the answer.

**Interactive practice (5 minutes):** Which sense helps you notice that the cloth is soft?

**Answer and feedback:** Touch, when safe and optional. Invite the learner to explain the answer in their own words, point to a drawing or respond through an assistive communication method. If unsure, revisit the model rather than marking a permanent failure.

**Printable worksheet (10 minutes):** Describe two objects using sight and another safe sense. Include a labelled picture or two sentences explaining the choice; allow dictation or oral response.

**Facilitator check:** Can the learner identify the key feature and explain a reason? Record only an anonymous classroom-level observation if permitted; no learner names or accounts required.

**Safety and accessibility:** Never taste unknown objects or touch sharp or hot items. Provide high-contrast text, spoken instructions, rest breaks and a paper alternative. All participation is optional and supervised.

**Extension:** Ask the learner to invent one additional safe example and explain how it connects to the lesson aim.

## Term 3 · Lesson 1: Keeping our surroundings clean

**Learning aim:** Explain why a clean classroom supports health.

**Preparation (5 minutes):** The facilitator reads the original scenario aloud, offers a large-print copy or illustration, and invites learners to describe what they notice. Avoid assessing speed, speech or handwriting.

**Model and discussion (10 minutes):** The classroom floor has scraps of paper after an activity. An adult provides a bin. Invite a learner to restate the observation, and model a respectful response to a different explanation. Ask which evidence supports the answer.

**Interactive practice (5 minutes):** What should pupils do with the paper scraps?

**Answer and feedback:** Put them in the appropriate bin with adult guidance. Invite the learner to explain the answer in their own words, point to a drawing or respond through an assistive communication method. If unsure, revisit the model rather than marking a permanent failure.

**Printable worksheet (10 minutes):** Draw a before-and-after clean classroom scene. Include a labelled picture or two sentences explaining the choice; allow dictation or oral response.

**Facilitator check:** Can the learner identify the key feature and explain a reason? Record only an anonymous classroom-level observation if permitted; no learner names or accounts required.

**Safety and accessibility:** Do not handle broken glass, sharp waste or unknown materials. Provide high-contrast text, spoken instructions, rest breaks and a paper alternative. All participation is optional and supervised.

**Extension:** Ask the learner to invent one additional safe example and explain how it connects to the lesson aim.
