Early Years: Counting at Home

Development sample · Ages approximately 3–5 · 15–20 minutes · Adult supervision required

Learning goal

Help your child recognise quantities from one to five through playful, everyday objects. Adapt the activity to your child's interests and pace.

Materials

Five safe, large household objects (for example, plastic cups or blocks), paper and a pencil. Avoid coins, beads or other choking hazards.

Parent teaching steps

  1. Warm-up (3 minutes): Count aloud from one to five together using fingers.
  2. Show (5 minutes): Place one cup on the table and say “one”. Add cups one at a time, counting together up to five.
  3. Try (5 minutes): Ask the child to give you two cups, then three. If needed, model the answer without pressure.
  4. Play (5 minutes): Arrange objects in groups and invite the child to count each group.
  5. Close (2 minutes): Celebrate effort and ask which number was their favourite.

Simple practice worksheet

Draw one circle. Then draw two circles. Then draw three circles. Ask the child to count each group with you.

Parent observation

Can your child count three objects with help? Can they show two objects independently? Record observations privately on paper; this sample does not collect children's data.

Teaching notes

Repeat on another day if the child is interested. Do not use this activity as a formal developmental assessment. This sample is not yet mapped or approved against a specific national curriculum.
