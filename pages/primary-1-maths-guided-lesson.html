Primary 1 · Mathematics · Guided sample lesson

Counting, Adding and Taking Away

Learn using everyday objects, follow worked examples and practise at your own pace.

Development lesson awaiting teacher and curriculum review. Original sample content only; partner resources are not yet imported or verified. No account or scores are saved.

Step 1 · Learning goals

What we will learn

  • Count objects up to 20.
  • Combine two small groups to find a total.
  • Take away objects to find how many remain.
  • Explain answers using objects, drawings or words.

Materials: 10 bottle tops, beans or pencils; paper and a pencil. Keep small objects away from children who may put them in their mouths.

Step 2 · Watch the method

Worked example: Addition

Put 6 bottle tops on the table. Add 4 more. Count all the tops together.

● ● ● ● ● ●   +   ● ● ● ● = 10

We started with six and added four. There are ten altogether. We write 6 + 4 = 10.

Step 3 · Try together

Worked example: Subtraction

Start with nine pencils. Put two away. Count the seven that remain.

9 − 2 = 7

Subtraction means taking away from a group. Ask the child to explain what changed and count the remaining objects.

Step 4 · Explore

Shapes and number order

A triangle has three sides. A square has four sides. Look for both shapes around your home or classroom.

Count aloud from 10 to 20. Ask which number comes after 14. Use a number line or counters for support.

Step 5 · Practise

Answer ten questions

Step 6 · Reflect

Show your understanding

Draw two groups of objects. Tell someone how many there are altogether. Then cross out some objects and explain how many remain.

Facilitator check: Can the child count accurately, combine groups and explain taking away?
